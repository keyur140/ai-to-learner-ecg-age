LESSON: How an expert AI tells younger (under 40) from older (65+) adults on an ECG.
The AI relies on these measurements, most important first:
1. QT interval: in older adults it tends to be lower (cut-point about 392 ms; higher points to younger).
2. Q-wave depth, lead III: in older adults it tends to be higher (cut-point about 0.00 mV; lower points to younger).
3. P-wave axis: in older adults it tends to be higher (cut-point about 51 deg; lower points to younger).
4. QRS duration: in older adults it tends to be lower (cut-point about 93 ms; higher points to younger).
5. T-wave height, lead V5: in older adults it tends to be lower (cut-point about 0.22 mV; higher points to younger).
6. P-wave duration, lead II: in older adults it tends to be lower (cut-point about 110 ms; higher points to younger).
7. Heart rate: in older adults it tends to be higher (cut-point about 72 bpm; lower points to younger).
8. R-wave height, lead V5: in older adults it tends to be lower (cut-point about 1.33 mV; higher points to younger).
No single cue is decisive. Count how many cues point to 'older', giving more weight to the cues near the top of the list, and decide by the balance.
