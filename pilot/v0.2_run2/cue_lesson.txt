LESSON: How an expert AI tells younger (under 40) from older (65+) adults on an ECG.
The AI relies on these measurements, most important first:
1. PR interval: in older adults it tends to be higher (cut-point about 157 ms; lower points to younger).
2. T-wave height, lead II: in older adults it tends to be lower (cut-point about 0.22 mV; higher points to younger).
3. S-wave depth, lead V1: in older adults it tends to be higher (cut-point about -0.78 mV; lower points to younger).
4. T-wave height, lead V1: in older adults it tends to be higher (cut-point about 0.00 mV; lower points to younger).
5. R-wave height, lead aVL: in older adults it tends to be higher (cut-point about 0.40 mV; lower points to younger).
6. QRS axis: in older adults it tends to be lower (cut-point about 33 deg; higher points to younger).
7. ST level, lead V5: in older adults it tends to be lower (cut-point about -0.02 mV; higher points to younger).
8. QTc (Bazett): in older adults it tends to be higher (cut-point about 428 ms; lower points to younger).
No single cue is decisive. Count how many cues point to 'older', giving more weight to the cues near the top of the list, and decide by the balance.
